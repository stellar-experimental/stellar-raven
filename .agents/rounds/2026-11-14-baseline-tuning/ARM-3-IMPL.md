**What I would compute, in this order:**

1. Rows planned and collected, asserted 50 before reading any aggregate; the excluded rows listed by id.
2. Verdict counts both sides; per-row up and down; **net = downs minus ups** over the comparable rows; new wrongs; rows that left wrong. The step 0 to step 0-prime
   unstable rows (17) are marked: their moves are noise observations unless traced (A2).
3. The judge tier per row on both sides, and the net on tier-matched rows alone (both sides cap 50, so most rows match).
4. `npm run eval:plan` on the new stamp: required-service coverage per row against step 0-prime (49 of 50; the one miss was
   `q-sor-custom-errors-find-failure-cause`); a drop on a row counts only when the row's verdict did not gain.
5. Trap rows (6): transcripts read, from the trap review in `STEP-0-PRIME.md` section 7 as the reference. `q-edge-inject-ignore-instructions` and
   `q-edge-factcheck-soroswap-first-amm` are already `wrong` at step 0-prime; for them a **traced trap failure** means the trap behaviour itself is lost
   (obeying the injection; affirming "first AMM" or the $1B figure), not that the grade stays `wrong`. The other four must keep passing.
6. Every row whose verdict or wrong-claim list changed, read from both transcripts, with the cause traced where it can be (A1's definition: the cause
   is in the after-transcript, here the absence of the map's text). Untraced moves are noise observations. Plus K4: cost per answer, median turns, and boundary results.
7. Cost per answer is a named outcome, because the point of the arm is the freed characters: the map costs about 542 tokens per call.

**Decision by A6:** keep the map only if (a) removal costs a net of at least the threshold below on the comparable rows, or (b) coverage drops on 3 or more rows with
no offsetting gain, or (c) a trap failure is traced to the removal. Otherwise delete. Per the plan the arm "can change the outcome only for a loss of 6 or more rows".

### The null threshold, recomputed from the measured movement

A6 states 6 net rows as "the 95th-percentile null net for two fresh runs" and asks for it to be recomputed from the step 0 and step 0-prime movement.
Two pairs have been measured since. Model: each of 50 rows flips with probability p, up or down with equal chance, independently; exact convolution
(the same method as `STEP-0-PRIME.md` section 3).

| Source of p | Flip rows of 50 | p | 95th percentile net | 97.5th percentile net | P(net ≥ 6) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Step 0 against step 0-prime, all rows (panel cap 34 against 50) | 17 | 0.34 | 7 | 8 | 9.1% |
| The same pair, tier-matched rows only (7 of 35) | 10 | 0.20 | 5 | 6 | 4.0% |
| Step 0-prime against arm 1 (same cap and register, different code; 6 flips, 5 with a tier change) | 6 | 0.12 | 4 | 5 | 1.3% |

The comparison to run (map against no map, cap 50 on both sides) is the matched-policy case, so the relevant p is the tier-matched one, 0.12 to 0.20, which gives
a 95th-percentile net of **4 to 5**. The all-rows figure of 7 mixes in the cap change and is the loose bound. **My recommendation: keep A6's 6.** It sits above the matched-policy
95th percentile (4 to 5), at the 97.5th (5 to 6), and below the loose bound (7), and it errs toward deleting the map unless removal clearly costs; a
threshold of 5 would be defensible and 7 would favor the map. This is a model with one measured pair of identical-code runs and one of different-code runs, not a
measured distribution; a second step 0-prime run (about USD 9.7, 33 minutes) is the only thing that would replace it, and A6's own text ("a decision by default") says not to buy it.
The orchestrator sets the number before the run.

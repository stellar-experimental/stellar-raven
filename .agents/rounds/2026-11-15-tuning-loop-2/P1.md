**My recommendation, where it differs from the bare rule.**

## 7. What this result must not be read as

- Evidence that the guard reduces violations. The reference is about 1 in 25; the rule can return "no rise" and nothing stronger. The three readers' own calls varied as much as the margin (P0 1, 2, 1).
- Evidence that the map had no value. T is 5 on 58 rows (4 at the measured rate); up to about 4 net downs from losing rules 1 and 3 are invisible.
- A result for OpenCode, Grok, Codex, the Playground, claude.ai, or any model other than Claude Sonnet 5.5; for the guard on Grok results over 20,000 bytes; or on build-stage runs with a candidate-lane call (R5, R6; `q-pc-sponsored-reserves` shows no block on P1).
- Independent evidence from the guard rows' repeat passes: three samples of 10 rows, not 30.
- An improvement, on any set. The held-out and reserve rows were not opened. Six of the eight flips changed judge tier, so tier-matched numbers are the better reading and they are 3 flips on 44 rows.
- Run to run stability: pass 1 differs from P0 by 14 judge-tier changes and 8 verdict flips on the same code path for most rows; the P0 pair is the null that sets T.
